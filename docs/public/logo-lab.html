The base

The geometry is settled

↓ Click any illustration on this page to download it as SVG. Alt-click for PNG at the size shown.

The traced silhouette is frozen. MARK_OUTER and MARK_HOLE are carried over unchanged, and nothing on this page edits them. Every variant below is a different rendering of those two paths — a fill rule, a palette, a treatment. If a variant needs the shape to change in order to work, that variant is wrong, not the shape.

That constraint is what makes a “forever” answer possible. A mark ages badly when the thing you committed to was a rendering — a gradient era, a long shadow era, a particular orange. It ages well when the thing you committed to was a silhouette, and the rendering was always understood as the replaceable layer.

the base
Where it came from

/assets/goforj-v7.png — the raster the silhouette was traced from. Kept for comparison only. The identity is the hexagonal cube, the notch that opens the G, and the counter cut through the front; the shading, wall gradients and drop shadow were rendering, and are gone.

The rule test

Three readings of the colour rule

The design system says colour means exactly one of four things, and that the form is load-bearing: action is molten orange and always filled, navigation is temper gold and always text or line. Action and navigation sit only 28.5° apart in hue, so filled-versus-line is carrying the distinction that colour cannot.

A logo has to declare where it stands relative to that rule, because the mark appears in the nav bar directly beside real links and real buttons. Below, the same frozen silhouette under all three readings.

What v1 did, unstated

v1's Quench direction filled the top faces with #FFC24D — that is --gf-nav, the navigation colour, used as a fill. It also hardcoded #7A3A2E, which is not a token in the system at all. Neither was flagged. The rule was broken quietly, which is precisely the erosion the handoff's closing caution predicts.

Variants

One shape, many renderings

Each card measures itself. Tones counts distinct fills and reports the smallest ΔL* between any two of them — below about 8 they merge at small sizes. Ground is the WCAG ratio of the dominant fill against the surface behind it. 1-bit reports whether the variant survives a hard threshold, which is what happens on an etched surface, a fax, or a single-colour print.

The face variants were warped, and it was a geometry bug

Every face-based direction in v1 rendered as a bent shard rather than a cube. The cause was not colour. v1's FACE_TOP, FACE_LEFT and FACE_RIGHT were extracted from luminance bands in the source PNG and never cleaned up, and they are broken: FACE_TOP's third subpath has an area of zero — it repeats the vertex 41.36 83.29, making a degenerate bowtie that paints nothing. FACE_RIGHT contains two zero-width spikes that double back on themselves, and one of its subpaths covers a region on the left of the cube.

Together the three groups accounted for 93.7% of the mark's area, and that figure counts overlaps as positive, so real coverage was lower. Parts of the mark were never painted; other parts were painted twice by faces that disagreed about who owned them.

They have been replaced. In an isometric cube the three visible faces meet at a single interior vertex, and the three edges radiating from it cut the plane into exactly three sectors — so the faces are now the frozen mark clipped to each sector rather than three separately traced outlines. This cannot warp by construction, and it measures at 100.00% coverage with 0.3px of overlap across 64,000 pixels, which is antialiasing on the shared edges and nothing else. The seam in Seam is drawn from those same three edges, so the seam and the face split can never disagree.

What the comparison is and is not evidence of

The current mark is a raster with a soft shadow and interior gradients, so below about 32px it is compositing detail it cannot resolve and goes muddy. That is a fair comparison at favicon sizes, because that is exactly what ships. It is not evidence that the underlying artwork is bad — the silhouette on this page was traced from it, and the geometry is the same geometry. The difference here is entirely rendering.

Measured

Where the counter closes

v1 asserted its small-size verdicts as hand-typed strings — holds, tones merge, grey smudge. This measures them. The mark is rasterised at each size, the aperture is identified as inside(outer) − painted, and the metric is how much body colour has bled into those pixels through antialiasing — reported as a WCAG ratio of aperture against body, so it is the same unit the rest of the system already argues in.

Because the geometry is frozen, this is a property of the shape itself and is identical for every variant. It answers one question — the minimum size this silhouette may ever be used at — and it answers it once, for good.

The seam, in device pixels

Seam's whole argument is that its detail disappears on its own rather than being swapped out, so that claim needs a number rather than a promise. The stroke is 2 units on a 112-unit viewBox, which fixes its width as a constant fraction of the mark.

Stated precisely, because “disappears” was too strong

A sub-pixel stroke does not vanish — the renderer paints it at reduced alpha. At 24px and 16px the seam is still faintly present as a softening of the top edge rather than as visible structure. That is the intended behaviour and it is why the ladder resolves smoothly, but “identical to the base at 16px” would be an overclaim. It is converging on the base, and it is visually indistinguishable at a glance from about 20px down.

The other ground

Light mode, with the light tokens

v1's “on white” strip rendered dark-mode marks on a white box. That is not a light-mode test — it is the mistake the handoff warns about, made on the page whose job was to catch it. These use the real html:not(.dark) token set read out of the stylesheet: accent darkens to #D6351A, and gold darkens to bronze at #8F5D00.

The bronze problem is a logo problem too

The handoff notes that gold has to darken to bronze in light mode to clear contrast, “which shifts the feel”. For UI that is a nuisance. For a logo it is disqualifying: a mark whose colours have to be redefined per mode is two marks. Any variant depending on gold cannot be the primary.

Loader

The one place a logo may move

Seam resolves the mark into three faces meeting at a single interior vertex. That is already the structure of a loading indicator — three parts, one centre, a natural order to travel in — so a loader here is not a new drawing. It is the same frozen silhouette with one of its faces leading.

This matters more than it sounds. Most product loaders are a generic spinner that happens to sit near a logo. This one is the logo, so the moment a page is waiting is still a moment the mark is on screen.

Gated twice, and the static state is the mark

The handoff requires every animation to use the prefers-reduced-motion + [data-gf-motion] pattern, so nothing here animates unless both permit it. The button above is an explicit opt-in for previewing; it does not override anyone's OS setting silently.

The important part is what a reduced-motion user sees: not a frozen spinner, but the mark itself. Every loader below degrades to a legitimate static logo, because the animation only ever changes which face leads — never the geometry.

In context

A loader is judged inline at 16–24px beside text, not at 64px on a swatch board.

Fill, driven by a real value

Fill is the only determinate loader here. Because the level is a transform on a clipped rect, it can be set to an actual percentage rather than looped — the mark becomes the progress bar instead of sitting next to one.

Which survive small

The same constraint that decided the variants applies here. A loader that signals by tone is a multi-tone mark for as long as it is animating, so it inherits the merge problem — measured below as the ΔL* between a leading face and a trailing one, composited over the ground.

Where it actually ships

The nav bar, at real size

This is the mark's highest-traffic appearance by an enormous margin, and it is roughly 24px. v1 never rendered it in context.

Lockup

Corrected to the system's display voice: Space Grotesk 700 at -0.045em. v1 set the wordmark at -0.03em, which is the h3 step, not the display step — so the wordmark was quietly off-system. Mark height equals cap height; the gap is 0.42× the mark.

Favicon ladder and app icon

The mark is not square, and v1's framing assumed it was

The bounding box measures 84.01 × 100 — an isometric cube seen corner-on is taller than it is wide by the projection ratio. v1 framed it in a square -6 -6 112 112, which pads correctly on the top and bottom and incorrectly on the sides: 6 units of gap on the left, 21.99 on the right. Every mark on that page sat 8 units left of centre, about 7% of its own width.

Against a flat ground that is invisible, which is how it survived. Behind a plate it is obvious, because the plate gives the eye two symmetrical edges to measure against — which is exactly why the tiles and circles looked wrong while the bare marks looked fine. Every frame here is now derived from the measured bounding box, and optical size is set by one number: the mark's long side as a fraction of the frame.

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space is one counter-width on every side — derived from the mark rather than picked, so it scales automatically and cannot be argued with.

    The trade, stated plainly
    

      The multi-tone variants are the most ownable and the most on-system, and
      they are the ones that fail every test in the Forever section — they
      collapse at 1-bit, they lose their meaning under a palette swap, and they
      need a second definition for light mode. The flat variants survive
      everything and say less.
    

    

      v1 proposed resolving this by shipping an isometric primary with a
      separate flat favicon. That is two marks with two maintenance paths, and it
      is how identities drift. The alternative worth taking seriously is a single
      mark whose detail is sub-pixel at small sizes, so it degrades into
      the flat version on its own rather than being swapped for it.
